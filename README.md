# Arabic-new-classifier-related-to-Qatar-

Arabic is the first language of more than 400 million people, and one of the most under-represented languages in modern AI. Arabic accounts for well under one per cent of web text, and Arabic models still trail their English equivalents on comprehension, dialect handling and cultural nuance.

Qatar has made closing that gap a national priority. Qatar National Vision 2030 rests in part on human development: building a capable and motivated workforce, investing in high-quality education and training, and creating accessible opportunities for life-long learning so that everyone can reach their full potential. Applied AI skills, built on real Arabic data, are now part of what that means in practice.

There is a practical problem behind this challenge too. Qatar appears constantly in Arabic-language news across the region, but appearing is not the same as being the story. An article about a regional summit may mention Qatar once in a list of attendees. Another may be entirely about Qatari policy without using the country's name in the headline. Telling those two apart at scale is the difference between useful media intelligence and noise, and it matters for anyone tracking how Qatar is reported: ministries, newsrooms, archivists, researchers and communications teams.

The objective of this challenge is to classify Arabic news articles across six independent dimensions at the same time: relevance of the news article to Qatar, plus five additional categories.
